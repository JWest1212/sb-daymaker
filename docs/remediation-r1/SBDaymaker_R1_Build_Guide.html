What was actually wrong

Most of what the audits saw traces back to four chains. Fixing the chains fixes dozens of findings at once.

Saves were being deleted. Finished events were never retired, so the catalog grew to 1,529 listings. The database only hands back 1,000 at a time. The cut fell on every evergreen place, and the Saved page deletes anything it cannot see. Every heart in the Discover guides erased itself.

The day's pick had no rules. A flag meant to gate it was set to true on every row, and the fallback picked the first thing in the list. A city planning hearing became the recommendation of the day.

The site did not know where things were. Two different area systems, 38 percent of listings with no area, and a Place door that only re-sorted. Plan and Near Me ignored your choice for the same reason.

The pipeline published raw data. Pipe-delimited titles, blurbs that repeated the title, a car photo on a football party, a food bank classified as lunch, and 619 rows that were really repeat sessions of the same weekly event.

The route

Three runs. Inside a run, Claude Code keeps going on its own. The flags are the only places it stops and waits for you.

Run A: Trust

Stop the damage, then clean up the cause. Saves must be made safe before dead events are archived, or the cleanup would delete more.

Wave 1

Stop the loss

What changes
Saved looks up each saved item directly and never deletes. The catalog query stops cutting off at 1,000 and leaves out finished events. The share link always shows on screen with a copy button. The backup form appears from the first save. Caches refresh after every nightly ingest.
You'll notice
Saving from a guide or a detail page sticks. The Month view suddenly has all the restaurants, museums, and regulars back. Sharing on a laptop shows the link.
Wave 2

Retire the dead, gate the pick

What changes
Events archive a week after they end and their pages stay reachable with an "already happened" banner. City meetings get a civic flag and leave the feed, search, and Plan. The day's pick needs an address, a real blurb, and a start time that has not passed. "Gray day move" only appears on truly gray days. The nearby list stops repeating one title.
You'll notice
The pick is something you would tell a friend about. Today's list is leisure, not agendas. Detail pages show a year when the date is not this month.
Checkpoint 1: archive about 1,100 finished events

You see counts by month and 20 example titles before anything moves to archived. Archived is reversible.

Checkpoint 2: which listings can be the pick

You see how many rows lose pick eligibility under each rule, with 20 examples, before the flag is written.

Run B: Foundations

Fix what the screens depend on before the screens are rebuilt: Plan's logic, one area vocabulary, and the pipeline that writes the words and pictures.

Wave 3

Plan keeps its promises

What changes
Food banks and library stops are not lunch. A thin day always gets an honest note. A chosen area is honored, or the plan says it widened. Dates work up to a month out. Badges stop welding onto words ("Cookingdinner"). "A handful of taps" becomes "Seven quick questions".
You'll notice
The audit's couple-on-foot-wanting-lunch plan returns a real afternoon.
Wave 4

Where things are

What changes
One list of eight areas with one spelling each, used by Explore, Plan, Saved, detail pages, and the digest. The Place door becomes a real filter; empty areas show as disabled with a count. Listings without an area get one from the venue, coordinates, or address. "Use my location" times out politely.
You'll notice
Choosing Funk Zone shows the Funk Zone. Near Me on Saved visibly reorders. "OTHER" and "Santa Barbara, Santa Barbara" disappear.
Checkpoint 3: fill in missing areas

You see a table of proposed area assignments by source and 30 examples before the backfill writes. Nothing is guessed; unresolved rows go to your sweep console.

Wave 5

Pipeline and content quality

What changes
Titles are cleaned on import (no pipes, "LOTG" becomes "Library on the Go"). Repeat sessions get a series key so they can show as one card. Cross-source duplicates merge. The nightly blurb rules require saying what a thing is, never repeating the title, matching the listed day, and no filler words. Prices get a note and a key. Outbound buttons say where they go. Events use the category motif instead of a random Wikimedia photo. Every image has alt text. The digest sample renders from this week. The guides' copy errors and the "wrong on purpose" line are fixed.
You'll notice
Cards read like a local wrote them. The digest sample is current. No more cars on football parties.

Run C: The screens

Rebuild the front door on clean data, close the loops around Saved and share, then align every name and update the canon.

Wave 6

Explore shell and cards

What changes
Filters and the time range live in the address bar, so back works and views can be shared. The six locked WHEN pills, including Tomorrow. One card design for every range, carrying area, time, and price in the same order, with series shown as "Every Sunday, next Sep 27". The whole card is tappable. No sideways scrolling on phones, everything reachable at 200 percent zoom, 44px tap targets. Search tolerates a typo and dedupes. Readable links everywhere, a branded 404, the guide-page error fixed, and load time remeasured.
You'll notice
This is the visible rebuild. The homepage feels like one app instead of three card styles.
Checkpoint 4: the new homepage

You see the homepage at phone width in Today, Weekend, and Month after the card rebuild, before the layout and search work continues.

Wave 7

Saved, share, offline, forms, metadata

What changes
Badge and list counts agree. "Did you make it?" says "This morning" for a morning event. The empty Saved page links somewhere. Recipients of a shared link see the wordmark and a line explaining what this is. Saved works offline. Forms validate in the site's own style and set expectations. Confirmation emails use the digest template. Every page previews with its own title and image.
You'll notice
Sharing a link to a friend looks like it came from a real product.
Wave 8

One word per idea sitewide: Area, Occasion, Activity, Reset, Evening, and one newsletter cadence line. Copy stops promising a map or a memory the product does not have. Nothing surprises you twice. The docs match the site.

What runs without a checkpoint, and why that is safe

Wave 5's data passes (title cleaning, series keys, duplicate merges, blurb re-enrichment, image re-resolution) run on Claude Code's own review rules and their samples land in the run summary. They are reversible: titles keep their slugs, merged duplicates are archived not deleted, and images can be re-resolved. The blurb re-run is capped at a $5 estimate; above that it is logged as blocked and skipped for you to decide. Wave 3's small recategorization of five rows is listed in the summary. Everything else is code, which each wave commits separately so any wave can be reverted on its own.

If something goes wrong

Every wave is its own commit on the remediation-r1 branch, so any wave can be undone without touching the others. Nothing deploys to production until you merge. If Claude Code reports a task as Blocked, it has skipped it and moved on; the run summary lists what would unblock it. If a checkpoint's evidence looks wrong, say "adjust" and describe what is off in plain words; the spec tells Claude Code to redo the dry-run.
